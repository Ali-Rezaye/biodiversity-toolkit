Compare checklists

Load two or more checklists to measure how much of their flora they share. The indices use presence and absence alone, which is what a checklist actually contains. Begin at order level and work down: at species level distant regions share very little, while neighbouring ones often share a great deal — and that contrast is itself the result.

Ordering reference

Use your own checklist

Names are used as supplied. For species-level validation, resolve them first against ChecklistBank or the GBIF matcher — this tool places and displays names, it does not verify them.

For verification: the arrangement follows APG V (Byng et al. 2026) for angiosperms, PPG I (2016) for ferns and lycophytes — sequence verified by C. Rothfels, July 2026 — Yang et al. (2022) for gymnosperms, and BPG (Li et al. 2024) for bryophytes, whose three phyla are sequenced hornworts → liverworts → mosses following Zhang et al. (2020). Each group’s source and status are shown beside its heading and travel with every exported figure. Every angiosperm row also carries its APG IV number as a crosswalk to the previous system. Within an order the published linear sequence is followed exactly, and quantity never affects position. Families are placed by name against this reference, by recognised former name where one applies, or by genus using a Catalogue of Life genus-to-family map; every substitution is reported rather than applied silently.

The “% of world” view uses accepted-species counts derived from Catalogue of Life (COL26.7 XR, version 2026-07-17, doi:10.48580/dgykv, CC BY) for every vascular family and for mosses, liverworts and hornworts. Where Catalogue of Life recognises segregate families that the adopted classification includes in a broader family, their species are aggregated into the accepting family and the row is annotated. Nine families have no count; they are drawn hollow rather than as zero. Any figure can be overridden per family with a global column in an uploaded CSV. A low share may reflect genuine under-collection or natural biogeographic absence; the interpretation is the specialist’s.

The subfamily view is data-driven: fern and lycophyte subfamilies come from the PPG community taxonomic database (pteridogroup/ppg, retrieved 29 July 2026), which its maintainers state is not yet PPG II. Angiosperm subfamilies are deliberately not loaded. Any family without a loaded treatment is shown whole.
